HOW TO READ
A CERTIFICATE

Anyone can print a certificate. What gives one meaning is the chain of independent assessment behind it — and that chain is what makes a certificate issued in Dhaka acceptable to a buyer in Frankfurt.

  1. The standard sets the requirements

    ISO publishes the standard — ISO 9001 for quality management — but certifies no one itself. The standard defines what an organisation's systems must demonstrably do.

  2. An accreditation body assesses the certifier

    UKAS in the United Kingdom, ANAB in the United States, CNAS in China, Inmetro in Brazil. Each is assessed against ISO/IEC 17011 and is a signatory to the international mutual-recognition arrangement, which is what carries a certificate across borders.

  3. A certification body audits the organisation

    The accredited certifier — assessed itself against ISO/IEC 17021-1 — audits the company on site, then issues a certificate for a three-year term with annual surveillance audits in between.

  4. The chain can be checked by anyone

    Break any link — an unaccredited certifier, or an accreditation body outside the arrangement — and the certificate carries no international standing. Accredited management-system certificates can be verified publicly on IAF CertSearch by company name or certificate number.
